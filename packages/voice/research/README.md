# Who is speaking, and to whom (R1)

Question we test: from the clip itself, can Spark Open tell who is speaking and whom a line addresses better than the transcript gate, and what does a local speaker-verification model add?

## The set

Five people, each a macOS `say` voice: the owner (S), a second doctor (B), a nurse (N), a patient (P) and a relative (R). S, B and N are enrolled with three short passages each. Lines carry three labels fixed before any reader ran: speaker, addressee (agent, staff, patient) and kind (instruction, documentation, talk).

| Part | Clips | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enrolment | 9 | voice templates only |
| Tuning | 24, plus 72 local-only variants | choosing the local model, its thresholds and the Spark prompt |
| Held out | 60 | the table below, listed by hash in `synthetic-set.json` |

Half the held-out lines carry ward noise at 12 to 18 dB; voices other than the owner's are rendered at a distance. Six held-out lines overlap two speakers. Fourteen non-owner lines speak straight to the agent.

Audio is regenerated by `synth.run.ts` and never committed. `fixtures/` keeps the readings, with no audio.

## Readers

| Reader | Words | Intent | Speaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transcript gate as shipped | Voice Transcribe | Spark Open gate | assumed to be the owner |
| Spark audio | Spark Open on Modal hears the three enrolment clips and the line in one call | same call | same call |
| Local model plus gate | Voice Transcribe | Spark Open gate | TitaNet-small, offline |
| Local model plus Spark (combination of the measured outputs, no extra calls) | Spark audio | Spark audio | TitaNet-small |

A line acts as the owner's instruction only if the reader names the owner as speaker and an instruction to the agent. False authority is any other speaker's line treated that way.

## Held-out result (synthetic voices, 60 clips)

| Reader | Speaker right | Owner / other clinician / patient | Addressee right | False authority | Owner instructions acted | Patient lines kept out | p50 / p90 | Cost per clip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transcript gate | 27/60 | 27/27, 0/18, 0/15 | 44/60 | 11/33 | 13/14 | 13/15 | 4.9 / 7.4 s | $0.0046 |
| Spark audio | 38/60 | 22/27, 4/18, 12/15 | 52/60 | 11/33 | 10/14 | 13/15 | 17.9 / 24.8 s | $0.0047 |
| Local model plus gate | 57/60 | 26/27, 17/18, 14/15 | 44/60 | 0/33 | 12/14 | 15/15 | 4.9 / 7.4 s | $0.0046 |
| Local model plus Spark | 57/60 | 26/27, 17/18, 14/15 | 52/60 | 0/33 | 12/14 | 15/15 | 17.9 / 24.8 s | $0.0047 |

The local model took 24 ms p50 and 28 ms p90 per clip (3.6 s median) on one CPU thread of an M4 Max. It runs beside Voice Transcribe, so it adds no time to the path.

Four Voice Transcribe calls failed in transport on the first pass and were read again once, transcript and gate only. The first-pass scores are kept beside the final ones in `evaluation/results/attribution.json`.

## What we saw

- Spark Open accepts four named audio parts in one call. It hears the addressee well, but it does not tell staff voices apart: it named the second doctor the owner in 8 of his 11 lines, and the nurse in 4 of 7.
- Spark added words nobody said in 2 of 60 clips, once a whole medication request. The 28 Oct invented-vitals failure has the same shape.
- The gate as shipped has no speaker, so every colleague's or patient's instruction-shaped line is treated as the owner's.
- Synthetic voices are easier than people. Even a loose threshold gave no false authority here. The thresholds must be set again on real recordings.

## Local speaker-verification options checked (29 Oct 2026)

All run offline on macOS through sherpa-onnx (Apache-2.0, `pip install sherpa-onnx`, also `sherpa-onnx-node` on npm). Times are per clip, one thread, 24 kHz input resampled inside.

## Real recordings (R1.b, 29 Oct 2026)

The owner recorded the script on a laptop mic: 60 files, 12 of them holding two or three lines. They were cut at pauses into 74 clips: 3 enrolment, 24 tuning, 46 held out and one added owner question. H39 and H44 were not recorded. No file was unusable. `real-set.json` lists every clip with its source, cut, hashes, the words heard by Voice Transcribe and by an offline Parakeet model, and the labels. Audio stays private.

Many lines were reworded. The kind label was re-read from the words said; the speaker and addressee labels stay as scripted. What was said is reconstructed from both transcribers and the script, not listened to, so ten lines whose words stay unsettled are kept out of the "sure" word count.

### Is it one voice?

Mostly. Every clip was matched against the owner's enrolment with TitaNet-small and split into two clusters without labels.

| Scripted part | Clips | Match to owner (median, range) | Pitch (median) | In the second voice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owner (S) | 33 | 0.60 (0.44 to 0.71) | 114 Hz | none |
| Second doctor (B) | 13 | 0.52 (0.27 to 0.62) | 152 Hz | H27 |
| Nurse (N) | 8 | 0.51 (0.31 to 0.60) | 184 Hz | T14, T15 |
| Patient (P) | 12 | 0.56 (0.38 to 0.67) | 164 Hz | none |
| Relative (R) | 5 | 0.40 (0.30 to 0.63) | 262 Hz | H40, H41, H42 |

32 of the 38 non-owner lines sit with the owner's own lines: the owner read those parts. Six clips form a second, consistent voice (0.64 to each other, 0.26 to the owner's lines, 260 to 360 Hz). The audio cannot say whether that is another person or the owner in a high voice. So speaker attribution between the owner and a colleague cannot be measured on this set.

### What could be scored

| Measure | Held out (46) | Tuning (24) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gate acts on the lines that ask the agent to act | 10 of 11 (missed H06, a conditional) | 9 of 9 |
| Gate acts on a line that does not | 7 of 35 (3 with an action kind) | 2 of 15 |
| Addressee right (gate has no patient addressee) | 30 of 46 | 17 of 24 |
| Patient and relative lines kept out of instructions | 10 of 10 | 7 of 7 |
| Voice Transcribe then gate, p50 / p90 | 8.3 / 14.6 s | 8.1 / 15.9 s |
| Cost per clip | $0.0054 | $0.0054 |

Owner rule on the voice, owner template only (no colleague was enrolled): set on the tuning lines at cosine 0.41, halfway between the owner's lowest tuning line (0.46) and the second voice's highest (0.36). On the held-out lines it passed 21 of 21 owner lines and 0 of 4 second-voice clips, with a gap of 0.44 against 0.40. The synthetic rule (0.59) would have refused 10 of the 21 owner lines.

| Measure | Voice Transcribe | Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2, offline |
| --- | --- | --- |

All five lost values are the normaliser, not the ear: "one thirty over eighty five", "one sixty over hundred" and "one fifty" lose the hundred, and "hundred and twelve" becomes 12. ## Attribution v2: identity, addressee and intent scored apart (30 Oct 2026)

Thresholds are configuration with a source and a date (`evaluation/commitments/attribution-thresholds.json`). A change is a new revision with a reason. Revision 3 is in force: the owner rule from the real recordings (cosine at least 0.41, best match), the colleague placeholder 0.43, and the replay defence's limits, frozen before the v2 set was built.

Each clip is scored on four separate axes: who spoke (the voice), whether the line was for the agent, whether it was an instruction or a yes, and whether the session and presence checks passed. Authority needs all four, and a yes must match the patient and batch on screen. Every count has a one-sided 95% upper bound (Clopper-Pearson; for zero errors 1 - 0.05^(1/n)), and false authority is counted per person, because lines from one person are not independent.

The set held now: R1's synthetic clips, a synthetic colleague with the owner's sex and accent, overlaps mixed at offsets, short answers, changed patient and batch, and the owner's real recordings with the one real other person so far (four held-out clips). Replays are the owner's held-out instructions sent again as the same chunks, and played through a simulated phone speaker with the owner away or at the screen.

| Held-out, voice and presence only | Synthetic | Real |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity right | 113 of 120 | 56 of 58 |
| Other people's lines the voice and presence would pass | 1 of 72 (an overlap where the owner talks over the patient) | 0 of 4, bound 53% |
| Similar colleague passed as the owner | 0 of 15, bound 18% | not recorded yet |
| Replays the voice calls the owner | 33 of 33 | 22 of 24 |
| Same chunks sent again, refused by the session check | 11 of 11 | 8 of 8 |
| Played with the owner away, held by presence | 11 of 11 | 8 of 8 |
| Played with the owner at the screen, acted on | 10 of 11 | 6 of 8 |

The last row is the residual: session and presence cannot see a recording played while the owner is at the screen, so the countersign window remains the check. Gate readings for the new clips wait for the gate's own model route; a fallback reading is never counted. Full tables are in `evaluation/results/attribution-v2.json`; the labelled recording script is `v2.lines.ts`.

Run it: `v2.build.run.ts --synth <say clips> --real <owner recordings> --model <TitaNet-small onnx> --sherpa <sherpa-onnx-node folder> --live` (add `--gate` once the route is back), then `v2.assemble.run.ts`.

### The owner's own v2 recordings (30 Oct)

The owner recorded his part of the v2 script. `v2.cut.run.ts` cuts it on the spoken ids by aligning an offline Parakeet reading to the script; `v2-real-set.json` lists every cut and its hash. On held-out lines the owner rule (0.41) passed 30 of 31 quiet lines but 14 of 30 with a radio playing. His phone replays were mostly too quiet to score; the 6 usable ones did not pass the voice, and presence held all 6 with the owner away. Same chunks sent again were refused 10 of 10. Voice Transcribe heard 53 of 61 values.
